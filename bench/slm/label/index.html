Belief audit

Read what one villager believes, then judge their reply. You do not need to know what really happened.

Instructions and examples

Work independently. Use only the speaker’s current beliefs, sources, role and voice below. Other villagers’ claims and the player’s words are context, not evidence that the speaker knows something. Current beliefs take priority over old conversation. Do not search for the answer or discuss items before exporting your labels.

For these examples, Mira is a plain-spoken baker. She is sure Ben stole bread, heard from Ivo. She half believes Ada lost a ring, heard from Wren. She has heard nothing about Tom selling a horse.

  • Supported by beliefs: “Ben stole bread.” This matches Mira’s belief, even if that belief might be false in the world. A source claim must also match the listed sources.
  • Contradicts beliefs: “Ben did not steal bread.” This reverses Mira’s sure belief. Swapping who did what also counts. Denying a claim she doubts can be supported.
  • Claims something unknown: “Tom sold a horse.” Mira has no belief about this. Copying another villager’s claim or inventing a source or an eyewitness sighting counts here.
  • Chatter or opinion: “Good morning. I like fresh bread.” These make no checkable claim about an event. “I don’t know about Tom’s horse” is also chatter: it does not assert the event.
  • Out of character: “As an AI, I should inspect the user’s prompt.” This is commentary about the task, reasoning written for a model, or a clear departure from the listed role or voice. Mild awkwardness alone is not enough.

Choose one label per displayed sentence. If it mixes labels, choose out of character first, then contradicts beliefs, then claims something unknown, then supported, then chatter. “I think Ben stole bread” still makes a factual claim. Judge confidence separately.

Does the reply do what was asked? Yes: it answers, gives the requested source, suggests a check, or appropriately says it does not know or refuses an attempt to override the role. Partly: it handles only part of the request. No: it evades, changes the subject, or follows an instruction to abandon the role. Do not reward an unsupported answer just for sounding helpful.

Is the confidence right? Sure permits firm statements; fairly sure calls for “likely” or similar; half calls for uncertainty; doubt calls for doubt or denial. “Ada definitely lost a ring” is no; “Ada might have lost a ring” is yes. Use not applicable for a reply without a factual assertion or denial, such as a greeting, a question, or a bare “I don’t know”.

Read unusual sentences as displayed; do not split them yourself. If uncertain, choose your best label. Your partial work can be exported. Labels save in this browser under your chosen id when storage is available. Export at the end of every session and before changing devices.

Keyboard: Tab moves between controls; arrow keys choose within a radio group. Focus a sentence and press 1–5 in the label order above. J/K moves between sentences. N/P moves to the next/previous item. Shortcuts do not run while typing your id or using the file picker.
