Rumourtown

How Rumourtown works

Rumourtown is an agentic browser game. Thirty-six villagers think for themselves; you are the stranger who came down from the sky and whispers a rumor. The village does the rest. It is free, needs no account, and works on phones. AI agents can play it too.

Play Rumourtown

What you do

Tap the ground to walk. Tap a villager to open their paper tag. Walk up to someone and whisper a rumor: a ready-made one, or type your own. Then follow the red thread, which shows who told whom in order, and read the story as sentences. Each evening you get the day's ending: reconciled, exposed, the village split, a grudge, or the rumor died. After three days a short debrief shows what you exploited and what it cost you.

How villagers think

Every villager is an autonomous agent with a routine, needs, moods with causes, friends and rivals, and a secret errand someone might witness. Beliefs keep a memory of who told them. Hearing something from a trusted friend moves belief about a third of the way; hearing it again makes it feel truer; three tellers who all got it from you only count as three if nobody remembers that it came from you. Seeing beats hearing. A correction lowers belief but never erases it, so a false accusation leaves hurt even after an apology. Villagers gossip to close friends about people they are less close to, take sides when someone is accused in public, and back an accusation only when they are fairly sure and have two sources. If a lie that traces back to you is exposed, the village trusts you less, for good.

Every rule comes from published behavioural research. The README lists each rule with its source and says which numbers are measured and which are tuned.

What the thread shows

The red thread is the path a rumor took: who told whom, in order. The story sheet tells the same path as sentences, and tapping a sentence takes you to the people involved.

Sharing

A link carries the seed and your whispers, so a friend replays the exact same three days on any device. Your typed words never travel; only the rumor the village understood does.

Your typed whisper and the budget

One small AI call reads your words and turns them into a village rumor: who it is about, what kind, how bad. It never writes what the villagers say and never decides what they believe. The service runs behind a fixed budget: prepaid credit, a capped key, and a ledger that reserves the cost before every call. When the day's allowance is spent, typed whispers rest and the ready-made ones keep working.

Why it exists

Rumourtown was made by Madhav, who likes to hear gossip, and wanted to see whether villagers built from real research would behave like people when a rumor runs through them.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. It runs in your browser with no account and no payment. Typed whispers use a small AI service behind a fixed budget; when that budget is spent, ready-made rumors keep working.

Does it need an account?

No. A share link carries everything needed to replay your three days exactly.

What makes it an agentic game?

Every villager is an autonomous agent with its own goals, routine, memory, beliefs, relationships and moods. Nothing is scripted, and AI agents can play through the published agent protocol.

Does the AI write what villagers say?

No. The AI only reads your whisper. The villagers' lines are authored, and the simulation decides what they believe and do.

Where do the rules come from?

From published behavioural research, listed rule by rule in the README with sources.

Can an AI agent play it?

Yes. The repository publishes an agent environment with observe, move, approach, wait and whisper tools, a JSON-lines bridge and exact replay.

Does it work on phones?

Yes. Tap to walk, a large whisper button, and a story sheet you can pull up.

Can I use the library?

Yes. The rumormind package gives any JavaScript game beliefs with provenance, trust, gossip decisions, side-taking and repair, with zero dependencies.
