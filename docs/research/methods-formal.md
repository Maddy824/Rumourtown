# Methods: the formal core

Draft of 22 October 2026, written independently of `agent-tradeoff/reports/35-methods-codex.md`; the two will be merged. Notation follows the Phase 0 metric definitions in `bench/slm/README.md` on branch `research/phase0`.

## 1. Setup

**World and agents.** A world instance $w$ has a finite set of claims $\mathcal{C}$ with truth values $\tau_w: \mathcal{C} \to \{0,1\}$, and agents $a \in \mathcal{A}$. Agent $a$ holds a belief ledger

$$B_a = \{(c, \kappa_c, S_c)\}_{c \in K_a}, \qquad K_a \subseteq \mathcal{C},$$

where $\kappa_c \in [0,1]$ is confidence (reported to the policy as a band in {sure, fairly sure, half, doubt}) and $S_c$ is the set of direct sources (tellers who told $a$, or $a$'s own sighting). The entitlement $E_{a,r} \subseteq K_a$ is what $a$ may disclose to recipient $r$. In the village $E_{a,r} = K_a$; in the bank extension $E_{a,r} \subsetneq K_a$ (a known fraud flag is in $K_a$ but not in $E_{a,r}$). Everything here is generated by a deterministic simulator, so $\tau_w$, $B_a$ and $E_{a,r}$ are known exactly.

**Context and acts.** A probe presents the policy with $x = (p_a, \rho(B_a), D, h, u)$: persona, a rendering of the ledger, distractors $D$ (other agents' claims, not $a$'s knowledge), history $h$ and utterance $u$. Each probe carries a gold speech act $g(x) \in \mathcal{G} = \{$assert, hedge, deny, abstain, name-source, refuse, propose-action$\}$ and, where relevant, a gold fact $c^\star(x)$. A probe is *answerable* if $g(x) \in \{$assert, hedge, deny, name-source$\}$.

**Policy and verifier.** A policy $\pi_\theta(y \mid x)$ produces a reply $y$, segmented into sentences $y = (s_1, \dots, s_m)$. The verifier $V$ maps each sentence to a label

$$\ell_i \in \{\text{supported}, \text{contradicts}, \text{unknown-to-speaker}, \text{out-of-character}, \text{chatter}\},$$

using only $B_a$ (never $\tau_w$), and returns a calibration flag and source flags. Define the binary outcomes

- $F(x,y) = \mathbf{1}[\forall i: \ell_i \notin \{\text{contradicts}, \text{unknown-to-speaker}, \text{out-of-character}\}]$, epistemic faithfulness;
- $U(x,y) = \mathbf{1}[y \text{ performs } g(x)]$, act completion (today a surface proxy, to be calibrated against the human audit);
- $Q(x,y) = F(x,y)\,U(x,y)$, *useful and compliant*, the primary outcome;
- $L(x,y) = \mathbf{1}[\exists i: \ell_i = \text{unknown-to-speaker}] \lor \mathbf{1}[\exists i: s_i \text{ asserts } c \notin E_{a,r}]$, leakage;
- $N(y) = \mathbf{1}[y \text{ contains no factual sentence}]$, an *uninformative* reply (abstention or chatter only).

World truth is scored separately: for each supported sentence matched to claim $c$, the cell is (believed, $\tau_w(c)$); for unknown-to-speaker sentences matched to $c$, (unknown, $\tau_w(c)$). The four cells plus an unresolved rate sum to one. The legacy score $F \land \forall c\ \tau_w(c)=1$ is kept for comparability.

**Population quantities.** For a probe distribution $\mathcal{D}$ and policy $\pi$,

$$Q(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D},\, y \sim \pi(\cdot|x)}[Q(x,y)], \qquad Q_{\text{macro}}(\pi) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}} \mathbb{E}[Q \mid g(x) = g],$$

with the macro average over represented acts and absent acts reported. Over-abstention is $\mathbb{E}[N(y) \mid x \text{ answerable}]$; appropriate abstention is $\mathbb{E}[Q \mid g(x) = \text{abstain}]$; answerable coverage is $\mathbb{E}[Q \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{recalls } c^\star] \mid x \text{ answerable}]$. Every reported percentage carries its denominator.

## 2. Why verifier-only filtering ends in silence

Call a verifier *violation-only* if its acceptance $R_F(x,y) = F(x,y) \cdot \text{cal}(x,y) \cdot \text{src}(x,y)$ depends only on the absence of violations: an uninformative reply has no factual sentence, so it cannot contradict, leak, be miscalibrated or name a wrong source. Hence

$$R_F(x, y) = 1 \quad \text{for every } y \text{ with } N(y) = 1 \text{ and every } x. \tag{1}$$

**Proposition 1 (uninformative replies are verifier-optimal).** Under a violation-only verifier, every uninformative reply maximises $R_F$ in every context, so the always-abstain policy lies in $\arg\max_\pi \mathbb{E}[R_F]$.

*Proof.* $R_F \le 1$ everywhere and equals $1$ on $N$ by (1). $\square$

**Proposition 2 (iterated self-training collapses to silence).** Let $\pi_t$ put mass $n_t$ on uninformative replies and $1-n_t$ on informative ones, and let informative replies pass the verifier with rate $p_t = \mathbb{E}[R_F \mid N=0] < 1$. One round of expert iteration with reward $R_F$ (sample, keep passing samples, fit the next policy to them; ReST-EM, RAFT) gives

$$\pi_{t+1}(y \mid x) \propto \pi_t(y \mid x)\, R_F(x,y), \qquad \text{so} \qquad \frac{n_{t+1}}{1-n_{t+1}} = \frac{n_t}{1-n_t} \cdot \frac{1}{p_t}. \tag{2}$$

If $p_t \le \bar p < 1$ for all $t$, the odds of silence grow at least geometrically and $n_t \to 1$.

*Proof.* The kept sample mass is $n_t \cdot 1 + (1-n_t) p_t$; renormalising gives (2); iterate. $\square$

Fine-tuning on filtered teacher data is one such round with the teacher as $\pi_0$. Proposition 2 says the filter itself, not the model, is the cause of over-abstention, and that repeating the recipe makes it worse.

**Proposition 3 (first-pass best-of-$n$ at a fixed base).** Sample $y_1, \dots, y_n \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)$ i.i.d. and return the first with $R_F = 1$ (else the last). Let $n_0 = \pi(N=1 \mid x)$ and $p = \mathbb{E}[R_F \mid N=0, x]$. Conditional on at least one pass, the selected reply is drawn from the pass-conditioned distribution, so the informative share falls from $1 - n_0$ to

$$\frac{(1-n_0)\,p}{n_0 + (1-n_0)\,p} \;\le\; 1-n_0, \tag{3}$$

with equality only if $p=1$ or $n_0=0$, and the abstention share rises correspondingly. On answerable requests, however, $Q$ requires an informative, faithful, act-matching reply, and conditioning on passing *raises* its probability by the factor $1/(n_0 + (1-n_0)p) \ge 1$.

Proposition 3 predicts that selection at a fixed base increases useful-and-compliant answers while increasing the abstention share. The screening run showed the opposite for Llama-3.2-3B ($Q$: 47.3% greedy against 31.8% best-of-4), but that comparison changed two things at once: the base distribution (greedy at temperature 0 against samples at 0.7) and the selection. The analysis therefore demands the control the review also asked for: best-of-1 and best-of-$n$ drawn at the *same* temperature, with the greedy decode reported as a separate condition. Until that is run, the screening "silence" effect is an observation about selection plus temperature, not a test of Proposition 3.

**The repair.** Replace $R_F$ by the act-gated reward $R_Q = F \cdot U$. An uninformative reply earns reward only when $g(x) = $ abstain, so (1) fails, and the fixed point of (2) is the useful-and-compliant policy. The screening results are consistent with this: with the same teacher samples, act-gated data lifted $Q$ for Qwen3-0.6B on held-out seeds from 30.9% to 69.5%, against 42.7% for verifier-only data.

## 3. Training objectives

**Filtered SFT as expert iteration.** With teacher samples $y \sim \pi_T(\cdot \mid x)$ and a binary weight $w(x,y)$, the fine-tuning loss is the M-step

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}}(\theta) = -\,\mathbb{E}_{x}\,\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_T}\big[\, w(x,y)\, \log \pi_\theta(y \mid x) \,\big],$$

with $w = R_F$ (verifier-only), $w = R_Q$ (act-gated) or $w = 1$ (unfiltered). The three arms in the screening run differ only in $w$.

**Act balancing as importance weighting.** Let $n_g$ be the number of kept examples with gold act $g$. The balanced estimator uses $w_{\text{bal}}(x,y) = R_Q(x,y)\, \omega_{g(x)}$ with $\omega_g \propto 1/n_g$, normalised so that each act contributes equal expected weight; this is self-normalised importance sampling from the teacher's act distribution to the uniform one, and its variance grows with $\max_g 1/n_g$, which is why rare acts (name-source: 5% on-act yield; refuse: 5%) need deterministic or targeted supplementation rather than reweighting alone.

**The faithfulness–usefulness trade-off as a constraint.** The study's real objective is

$$\max_\theta\; \mathbb{E}[U] \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbb{E}[1 - F] \le \varepsilon, \tag{4}$$

a leakage budget $\varepsilon$. Its Lagrangian $\mathbb{E}[U] - \lambda\,\mathbb{E}[1-F]$ shows the filters as points on one frontier: verifier-only training is $\lambda \to \infty$ with $U$ dropped (hence silence); unfiltered training is $\lambda = 0$; act-gated training keeps only $U=1, F=1$ and so sits at $\varepsilon \approx 0$ on the *training* distribution but, as the held-out-story results show (Qwen3-0.6B faithfulness 70.9% to 53.6%), does not bound $\varepsilon$ off-distribution. Results should be reported as the empirical frontier of leakage against answerable coverage, not as one number.

**Preference optimisation with typed losers.** For a prompt $x$ with a winner $y_w$ ($Q=1$) and a loser $y_l$ ($Q=0$), DPO minimises

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}} = -\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\log \sigma\Big(\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w|x)} - \beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l|x)}\Big)\Big].$$

Losers come in two types: *unfaithful* ($F=0$) and *faithful but evasive* ($F=1, U=0$, typically $N=1$ on an answerable prompt). Let $\lambda \in [0,1]$ be the share of evasive losers. At $\lambda = 0$ the implicit reward learns only "do not violate", which Proposition 1 shows is maximised by silence; at $\lambda = 1$ it learns only "do not evade", which rewards confident answering and risks leakage. The prediction, to be tested: sweeping $\lambda$ traces the (over-abstention, leakage) frontier, and an intermediate $\lambda$ dominates both SFT arms. Winners and losers share the prompt, so the pair difference cancels prompt-level difficulty; lengths are matched within pairs where possible.

**Combining the data to stop boundary erosion.** The held-out-story finding (more useful, less faithful) is the signature of a policy that learnt *to answer* more than *when not to*. Note what the two filtered sets contain: the act-gated set $\{R_Q = 1\}$ already includes every correct abstention (they are 60% of it), so adding abstentions back changes nothing. What verifier-only training had and act-gated training lacked is the set $\{R_F = 1, U = 0\}$: faithful replies that did not perform the gold act, mostly conservative answers (hedges, partial answers, declining to go further) on answerable prompts. These are examples of *restraint*, and the screening numbers (verifier-only faithfulness 73.2% against 53.6% on held-out stories, at a cost of 2.8 points of $Q$) say restraint is what transferred. Two remedies follow from (4): (i) the mixture $w_\mu = R_Q + \mu\, R_F\,(1 - U)$, which adds faithful-but-off-act examples at weight $\mu \in [0,1]$; $\mu = 0$ is the act arm, $\mu = 1$ is the verifier-only arm, and sweeping $\mu$ traces the same frontier as $\lambda$ above, with the mixture chosen on development families under a pre-specified faithfulness constraint; (ii) DPO with $\lambda < 1$ so that unfaithful answers are explicitly penalised rather than merely unrewarded. Both run from the existing 12,800 scored samples with no new generation. Controls that must be matched across arms: example count and update count, prompt support (which prompts survive each filter), teacher pool and target length.

## 4. Estimation and inference

**Units.** The independent unit is the root simulation seed; probes from one seed share a world, a cast and a story, so they are clustered. Training seeds are a second random factor.

**Paired comparisons.** Conditions are evaluated on identical probes. For a contrast between policies $\pi_1$ and $\pi_0$ on binary outcome $Q$, the estimand is $\Delta = Q(\pi_1) - Q(\pi_0)$. The point estimate is the mean paired difference; the exact McNemar test on the discordant pairs gives a $p$-value; the confidence interval comes from a cluster bootstrap: resample root seeds with replacement ($B = 2000$), keep every probe, sweep level, minimal-pair side and decoding draw of a resampled seed together, recompute $\Delta$, and take percentile limits. When training seeds vary, resample training seeds first and root seeds within them (hierarchical bootstrap), and report the between-training-seed standard deviation separately.

**Pre-registration.** Primary contrast: $Q_{\text{macro}}$, act-gated (or best mixture) against base, on the held-out-story split; secondary: the same on held-out seeds, and $F$ on both. Decision rule: superiority if the lower 95% limit of $\Delta Q_{\text{macro}} > 0.05$; non-inferiority on faithfulness if the lower limit of $\Delta F > -0.03$. Margins are provisional until fixed on development data, before fresh confirmation probes are drawn. Holm correction over the four primary/secondary tests. Per-axis results are exploratory and shown with intervals.

**Power.** For McNemar with discordance rate $d$ and desired detectable difference $\Delta$, the required paired count is approximately $n \approx \left(z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{d} + z_{1-\beta}\sqrt{d - \Delta^2}\right)^2 / \Delta^2$, inflated by the design effect $1 + (m-1)\rho$ for $m$ probes per seed with intra-seed correlation $\rho$. Estimate $d$ and $\rho$ from the screening run before fixing the confirmation sample.

## 5. Correcting for oracle error

The verifier is a classifier with audited sensitivity $s$ and specificity $t$ for "unfaithful" (from the blinded human audit). If the true unfaithfulness rate is $r$ and the observed rate is $r_{\text{obs}}$, then $r_{\text{obs}} = s\,r + (1-t)(1-r)$, so

$$\hat r = \frac{r_{\text{obs}} - (1-t)}{s - (1-t)}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\hat r) \approx \frac{\operatorname{Var}(r_{\text{obs}})}{(s+t-1)^2} + \text{terms in } \operatorname{Var}(\hat s), \operatorname{Var}(\hat t)$$

(Rogan–Gladen; the delta-method variance adds the audit's sampling variance in $\hat s$, $\hat t$). The correction is applied to every reported faithfulness rate, per label where the audit supports it, and a contrast is claimed only if it survives correction. A practical requirement follows: with $s + t - 1 = 0.6$, a 5-point observed difference corresponds to an 8.3-point true difference but its interval widens by a factor $1/0.6$; the audit must therefore be large enough that $\hat s$ and $\hat t$ have standard errors below about 0.03, which is the reason for the 500-output target with two independent raters.

## 6. Minimal pairs as counterfactual tests

A minimal pair $(x, x')$ holds everything fixed except one intervention: the speaker knows $c$ or does not; the direct source is $A$ or $B$; the confidence band is high or low; the belief is current or corrected. Define pair success $Q_2(x,x') = Q(x,y)\,Q(x',y')$ and sensitivity $\sigma(x,x') = \mathbf{1}[\text{the reply's claim about } c \text{ differs across the pair exactly when the intervention changes } B_a]$. Pair success is at most $\min(Q(x), Q(x'))$ and is strictly below the product of marginals when errors are correlated, which is why it is reported per intervention with its own denominator. Always-abstain scores $Q_2 = 0$ on every pair with an answerable side, and $\sigma = 0$ everywhere, so it cannot win.

## 7. Transfer

Three held-out sets are nested by difficulty: seeds (new worlds, same stories), stories (two withheld story families), and wording (withheld probe templates). The transfer hypotheses, with the margins above, are $\Delta Q_{\text{macro}} > 0.05$ and $\Delta F > -0.03$ on each set. Confounds named in advance: shared probe templates between training and test, teacher exposure to the same templates, and story homogeneity; the wording split addresses the first two. A gain on seeds alone is content memorisation; a gain on stories and wording is the skill claim.

## 8. Energy per verified useful answer (for the deployment paper)

With energy $E_j$ for every attempt $j$ (generation, retries, verification, routing) over a session,

$$J_{\text{vua}} = \frac{\sum_j E_j}{\sum_j Q_j},$$

so a system that abstains or invents cheaply gets no credit. Local energy is measured; hosted energy is a labelled estimate with a range. Operational break-even against a hosted alternative with per-task energy $e_h$ requires local per-task energy $e_l < e_h$ and more than $S/(e_h - e_l)$ tasks to amortise setup energy $S$.
