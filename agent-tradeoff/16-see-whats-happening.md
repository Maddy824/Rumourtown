# 16: See what's happening — focus, story rail, phone first

Owner feedback after task 14: "even now it's cluttered; I can't actually look into what's happening". On a phone the only story signal is a truncated one-line strip; the map is busy with labels; bubbles come and go. This task gives the rumor a readable home and strips the map down while a rumor is being followed. UI, renderer options and styles only; no simulation changes; replay pin unchanged. Read sections 4 and 11 of the design document, then `apps/rumortown/src/ui/` (app.ts, story.ts, story-beats.ts, bubbles.ts, name-labels.ts, timebar.ts, rumor-trail.ts, field-guide.ts, whisper.ts), `src/styles/app.css` and `src/render/index.ts` (RenderOptions).

## A. The story rail (the home of "what's happening")
1. Replace the running one-line strip with a **Now bar**: full sentence, up to two lines at 15 px, never truncated with an ellipsis; two small faces; a chevron. Tapping it opens the **story rail**: a right-hand paper rail on desktop (320 px, map stays interactive) and a bottom sheet on phones (half height by default, draggable to full, with the Now bar as its handle).
2. The rail lists story beats newest first as cards: time, faces, one plain sentence ("Mira told Ben that Tom stole the bread"; "Ben defended Tom"; "Mira apologised to Tom"; "The stranger's word is worth less now"). Each card has "Show me": the camera pans to the people involved, both get a spotlight ring, everything else dims to 40% for four seconds. Beats come from the engine events already used for timeline pins; small talk never appears here.
3. At the top of the rail, a **chain strip** for the rumor being followed: the faces in order of transmission with short arrows (The stranger → Mira → Ben → Rosa…), horizontally scrollable, each face tappable to jump the camera. It appears after the first whisper.
4. Day-end cards and the debrief open inside the rail, not over the map.

## B. Focus mode while following a rumor
5. "Follow a rumor" becomes a toggle in the Now bar once a rumor exists. On: small-talk bubbles hidden; only rumor-related speech shows; name labels only for people in the chain plus the six core villagers; the red thread draws the path; the camera follows each new transmission (with a 2 s hold) unless the player moves, in which case it stops following and shows "Following paused. Tap to resume." Off: normal view.
6. Hide the "Tap a path to walk. Gold is you…" hint permanently after the first tap or after 20 seconds, and never show it over the Whisper button.
7. Move the dare out of the main screen: it lives in the rail header as one line.

## C. Phone first (360–430 px wide, portrait and landscape)
8. Layout uses `100dvh`, safe-area insets, `touch-action: none` on the canvas (no page scroll, no double-tap zoom), a 64 px Whisper postbox at bottom centre with the Time control to its right, and a top strip that holds only the clock and the three pins; the Now bar sits directly under it.
9. At phone widths: one speech bubble at a time at full strength; home nameplates hidden; public-building signs only within six tiles of the player; district banner at 60% size; name labels only within three tiles plus the chain; the opening card fits without scrolling.
10. Tap targets at least 44 px; the whisper sheet's people list uses 56 px rows with faces; the rail's cards are full width.
11. Landscape phones: the rail becomes a right-hand drawer; the postbox moves to bottom right.

## D. In-app guide: "How it works"
12. Add a "How it works" page under More and as a link in the opening card, in plain words, five short sections with the opening-card glyphs: What you do (walk, whisper, follow the thread, read the tags, see the ending); How villagers think (beliefs with a memory of who told them; trust and repetition; corrections leave a residue; seeing beats hearing; they take sides and make up) with a note that every rule comes from published research and links to the README's rule table; What the thread shows (who told whom, in order); Sharing (the link replays the exact same days; typed words never travel); Your typed whisper and the budget (one small AI call reads your words and turns them into a village rumor; it never writes the villagers' lines; the service stops at a fixed budget and the game keeps working). Keep each section under 80 words.
13. Add the same text to `docs/how-it-works.md` and link it from the README.

## E. The arrival: loading page, opening guide (owner request)
14. **Arrival sequence as the loading page.** On every visit, before the village is interactive, show a full-screen felt-and-paper sky: a denim-to-oat gradient with the paper grain, three or four layered felt clouds. A small hooded felt figure (the stranger, drawn with the renderer's own sprite composition so it matches the in-game figure) falls from the top of the sky with a gentle tumble while the clouds part and the real village rises into view beneath (the actual map layers, so this doubles as asset loading), and lands in the square with a soft paper "puff" ring. Total length 2.5–3 s; a "Skip" paper tag from the first frame; any tap skips. Under reduced motion: a single cross-fade from the sky to the village with the figure already standing. The sequence must not block on the font or on zoom-layer preparation beyond 3 s; if assets are still loading when it ends, show the village with a small "Stitching the village…" paper note until ready.
15. **Title and the creator line** over the sky during the fall: "Rumourtown" in Bricolage Grotesque at the display size, then the subtitle "A village that gossips. You're the stranger who came down from the sky." and, smaller, "Made by Madhav, who likes to hear gossip." with the name linking to https://github.com/Maddy824. Sentence case, no exclamation marks.
16. **The guide opens on arrival.** After the landing, the "How it works" page from item 12 is what the visitor sees first: three swipeable paper cards (What you do; How villagers think; Sharing and your typed whisper), each with its glyph, under 60 words, with "Next" and a final "Walk in". "Show me" on the last card auto-walks to Mira and opens the whisper sheet. The visitor can skip at any card. Shown on every visit until the player has finished a day, then only when chosen from More ("How it works" and "Replay the arrival"). Store that one flag in localStorage with try/catch.
17. The arrival is one orchestrated motion moment; nothing else on the page animates on load.

## F. Say that it is an agentic game (owner request)
18. Visitors must learn, without digging, that Rumourtown is an agentic game: every villager is an autonomous agent with its own goals, routine, memory, beliefs and decisions, nothing they do is scripted, and AI agents can play the game too through the published agent protocol (`agent/environment.ts`, `npm run agent:demo`, documented under More → developer guide). Put this in: the arrival subtitle (one line, e.g. "Thirty-six villagers who think for themselves. You're the stranger who came down from the sky."), the first guide card, the rail's empty state ("Whisper something and watch the agents react"), and the About page. Plain words; no hype.
